Leave this leaf in the box and the game is complete without it. Used, the {{whispers}} words are
shuffled face down each night.

## Taking one

After the deal and **before any pledge is made**, each eligible player may look at their own hand
and then take one word or go without.

**The court does not confide in whoever is winning.** Only a player whose favour is _strictly less_
than the highest at the table is eligible. On the first night of a season the whole table is level,
so nobody is offered a word at all.

Taking one **costs nothing**, but it is taken **unread**: you decide on the strength of your hand
alone, not on the word you are about to receive. **{{burdens}} of the {{whispers}} are burdens**,
which cost rather than pay, so a word is a gamble rather than a formality.

That a player took one is plain for the table to see; _which_ one is not, until the night ends.
**The Watched** is the only word that breaks this rule. 

## What a word does

A Whisper bends how that player's favour is counted, or asks something of the agents they send, or
both. Its contents stay **private** until the night ends, when it is revealed alongside the
errands.

Some words count **the agent that took the audience for you** -- the one that beat the rest. Others
count **every agent in an audience you took**, whoever played it. Each card says which.

Three words set your own pledge aside altogether and score the night some other way, so their
holder is playing to a different end from everyone else at the table: **Sworn to the Fool**, **In
Another's Pay** and **The Understudy**. The pledge is still kept or broken as a fact, for the sway
ladder -- it is only the _scoring_ of it that is set aside.

## Demands

Five words ask something of your errands. **A demand is a request, not a rule.** You may always
pledge exactly as you please and ignore the word entirely.

But a word that was not heeded **pays nothing at all**: its reward is forfeit, and you score the
night as though you had gone without one.
